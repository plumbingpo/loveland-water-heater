A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so there is no standby loss while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water as it moves through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply for as long as you stay within the unit's rating, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Families across Loveland, CO often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

The most important step happens before anything is installed. We total your peak demand in gallons per minute, such as a shower, a kitchen sink, and the dishwasher at once, and compare it against the temperature rise your incoming water needs in winter. That tells us the real capacity you need. Next we check the supply side. Gas tankless units fire at up to around 199,000 BTU, so a half-inch line that fed an old tank is often too small. Condensing models vent through PVC or polypropylene and need a condensate drain, while non-condensing units need stainless steel venting. Electric models are their own case, often needing multiple large breakers and sometimes a service upgrade.

Every tankless install from Loveland Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, a clean gas connection with a sediment trap, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is the main enemy of a tankless heat exchanger. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wanting a unit that can last up to twice as long as a typical tank
  • An aging tank that has started to rust, rumble, or leak
  • Construction or remodeling that opens walls and simplifies the install
  • A utility area where a bulky tank takes up room you would rather use
  • More people or more fixtures than your current heater was sized for
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • Standby losses on a tank that sits idle for long stretches
  • A far-off sink or bath where a point-of-use unit could help

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Installation?

Buying the right unit is the easy part. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so the size of the gas line, the capacity of the meter, and the pressure at high fire all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are set by the manual and the code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Get any of these wrong, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

A professional install protects both performance and safety. We start with a real demand assessment, confirm the gas and electrical supply, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, followed by a full-flow test. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The goal is the right water heater, not just a tankless sale.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Descale the heat exchanger once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep the inlet screen clear so flow stays strong and the unit fires reliably
  • Treating hard water protects the heat exchanger from mineral buildup
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Make sure the outside vent and intake are never blocked
  • In freezing weather, keep the unit powered and insulate exposed piping
  • A setting near 120F limits scalding and reduces scale formation
