No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we start any repair, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and size of the tank, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is replacing a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment settles on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection right away. Catching it early often keeps a fixable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The date code on the label usually gives it away. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one forces the issue. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Annual Flushing

Maintenance Plans for Loveland Homes

A maintenance visit from Loveland Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and confirms a safe water temperature. Small problems are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your water heater keeps running at full efficiency.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. A tank in its later years needs a closer look than a brand-new one, a tankless unit on hard water needs regular descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a large family works the heater far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every service keeps the unit efficient and extends the life of the equipment you've already paid for.

The savings show up quickly. A flushed tank transfers heat more efficiently, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Sized for Your Household

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Loveland Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your morning rush, rather than simply copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute to the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Needs change over time. A growing family or an added bathroom can overwhelm the tank that used to be plenty. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while heat pump water heaters draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. If you're torn between tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll explain the real trade-offs in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Loveland

Homes around Loveland run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Loveland Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Not every water heater problem is equally urgent. A failed element only means lukewarm showers. A relief valve that discharges, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and a slow leak at the base can quietly ruin subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. Our technicians carry the parts to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
