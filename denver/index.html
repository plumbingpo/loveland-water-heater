Keeping Your Denver Water Heater Running Longer

Most premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
