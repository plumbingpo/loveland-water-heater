A cold shower from a gas water heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Across Loveland, CO, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Diagnosing a gas water heater properly means checking combustion, not just parts. Across Loveland, our technicians measure thermocouple output in millivolts, study the pilot and burner flame for color and shape, read gas pressure at the valve, and test draft at the hood so we know exhaust is leaving the house. We check the combustion chamber for soot and scorching, which point to a blocked flue or poor combustion air, and we test for carbon monoxide near the heater. Safety always comes first: a gas smell means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside before anything else, and Loveland Water Heater can take over from there.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. We replace thermocouples, flame sensors, pilot assemblies, and igniters, clean burners and flame arrestor screens, swap failed gas control valves, and correct vent connectors that have separated or lost their slope. We also flush sediment, the cause of that popping or rumbling sound and a major reason tanks overheat at the bottom and fail early. When the tank itself is leaking or the heater is deep into its service life, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. New installations are sized by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and include the right venting, a sediment trap on the gas line, an expansion tank where needed, and seismic straps and a drain pan where code requires them. Signs You Need Gas Water Heater Services in Loveland

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Two things make gas water heater work a poor DIY project: the parts involve fuel and combustion, and the symptoms overlap. A pilot outage might come from a worn thermocouple, a partly blocked pilot tube, lint on the flame arrestor screen, a downdraft, or an internal valve failure. A wrong guess there just wastes money. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
