Keeping Your Fort Collins Water Heater Running Longer

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Fort Collins Homeowners Face

Water heaters in and around Fort Collins face a familiar set of problems. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be a real factor: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Age is the other big factor. Many homes around here still have a tank that was installed a decade or more ago, and once a tank is past about ten years, the anode rod is usually spent and corrosion begins attacking the steel from the inside. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, which means we service both sides: thermocouples, burners, and venting on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Setup matters too. A home on a private well may see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless unit on hard water should get regular descaling. And an older installation getting a new heater often needs code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Give us a call at (970) 602-6532 to set up a visit.
