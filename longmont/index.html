Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Longmont

Most early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
