Water heater installation covers more ground than a simple swap. Some Loveland, CO homeowners are finishing a basement or building an addition and need a second heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. Each of those jobs involves new gas piping, new vent routes, new circuits, or new water lines, and each one has to be planned before the first pipe is cut. Loveland Water Heater handles installations of every size, from a single point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Good installations start on paper. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. Gas units need a way to vent outdoors and enough air for combustion. A heat pump model needs space, a room that stays reasonably warm, and a condensate drain. An electric tankless unit may need a heavy dedicated circuit and enough panel capacity to support it. Above finished space or in an attic, the heater needs a drain pan piped somewhere safe. We work through all of it with you in plain language, then give you a firm price.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. We run and pressure-test new gas piping, add a sediment trap, and route venting exactly to spec. Electric and heat pump units get a circuit matched to their load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Where code requires an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps, we add them, and we pull the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Then we fill the heater, confirm draft and combustion on gas models, and set a safe temperature. Planning a new installation? Call (970) 602-6532 for an assessment in Loveland.

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Installing a water heater from scratch involves gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines must be sized for the total load on the system and tested for leaks. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

With a professional install, every piece is done right once. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your demand, calculate the gas line, plan the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and install the code items your location requires. Leaks, draft, and carbon monoxide are tested, and the heater is left at a safe temperature with the paperwork handled. It is the gap between a heater that just works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts when the weather turns. And if a simpler option would serve you just as well, we tell you that too, without pressure.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our recommendations:

  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
