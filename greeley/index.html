Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Greeley

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
